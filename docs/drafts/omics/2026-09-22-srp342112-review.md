## 2. Claim 2, attacked hard — and it breaks

The claim was: *"Of the five constructs declared in Y797, only ILV3 raises its transcript,"* with
the supporting contrast that Y812's **truncated** ScILV5Δ2-48 under pTEF1 does show up while
Y797's **full-length** ScILV5 under the same promoter does not.

### 2.1 The framing is wrong before any mechanism is considered

ILV2 and ARO10 are not elevated in **any** of the three producers, at any timepoint. This is not a
Y797 peculiarity; it is study-wide. And the decisive fact the claim overlooks:

> **Y799 declares no ScILV5 whatsoever** — its KARI is *E. coli* `ilvC6E6` — **and Y799 shows the
> same ILV5 elevation as Y812**, at 4 h even slightly higher (Y799 4,848/3,325 vs Y812
> 3,584/3,595/3,945).

So ILV5 elevation tracks the **cytosolic-pathway strain class**, not the declared ScILV5 cassette.
Truncation is not the explanatory variable, and the Y797-vs-Y812 comparison that the claim rests
on is confounded.

### 2.2 The promoter-tracking test

A gene expressed from a heterologous promoter must hold a roughly constant cargo/promoter ratio
across conditions. Ratio of cargo CPM to the CPM of the gene the promoter was taken from:

| cassette element | strain | 4 h | 10 h | 26 h |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pPGK1-ILV3 → **ILV3/PGK1** | Y795 (none) | 0.052 | 0.006 | 0.023 |
| | Y797 | **0.247** | **0.177** | 0.156\* |
| | Y799 | **0.249** | **0.178** | **0.231** |
| | Y812 | **0.271** | **0.215** | **0.240** |
| pTEF1-ILV5 → **ILV5/TEF1** | Y795 (none) | 0.184 | 0.126 | 0.162 |
| | **Y797** | **0.146** | **0.109** | **0.148** ← indistinguishable from parent |
| | Y799 | 0.638 | 0.302 | 0.228 |
| | Y812 | 0.465 | 0.520 | 0.213 |
| pADH1-ILV2 → **ILV2/ADH1** | Y795 (none) | 0.065 | 0.064 | 0.087 |
| | Y797/Y799/Y812 | 0.043–0.046 | 0.056–0.131 | 0.105–0.112 |
| pTDH3-ARO10 → **ARO10/TDH3** | all four strains | **0.000** | **0.000** | 0.011–0.013 |

\* depressed by the mislabelled parent SRR16481346 sitting in the Y797 @ 26 h cell.

ILV3 holds a stable elevated ratio in all three producers while the parent's swings 9-fold —
the signature of a constitutive promoter. ILV5 in Y797 is **flat against parent**. ILV2 shows
nothing. **ARO10 shows nothing at all, in a strain where it is driven by pTDH3 — the strongest
promoter in the cassette (native TDH3 = 66,668 CPM at 4 h).** Expressed as "fraction of the
promoter's own output recovered on the native gene", at 4 h:

| cargo | promoter's own CPM | gain over parent | % of promoter output |
|---|---|---|---|
| ILV3 | PGK1 33,294 | +5,415 … +5,673 | **16.3 – 17.0 %** |
| ILV5 (Y797) | TEF1 6,900 | −110 | −1.6 % |
| ILV2 | ADH1 10,427 | −106 … −199 | −1.0 … −1.9 % |
| **ARO10** | **TDH3 66,668** | **+0** | **0.0 %** |

A cassette gene under the strongest promoter in the construct contributing *exactly zero* is not
a biology result. It is a measurement result.

### 2.3 The mechanism, settled against the deposited sequence

The paper's Methods: *"**Each open reading frame was codon-optimized**, synthesized commercially"*
(ILV2, ILV3, ILV5, ARO10 from *S. cerevisiae*; adhA29C8 from *L. lactis*). And in Results:

> *"Normalized counts (RPKM) for all synthetic codon-optimized isobutanol genes … were
> successfully detected … In general, the amount of RNA detected for each transcript correlated
> with the predicted promoter strengths of each gene (**P<sub>TDH3</sub>-ARO10 > P<sub>PGK1</sub>-ILV3 >
> P<sub>TEF1</sub>-ILV5/ilvC6E6 > P<sub>TEF2</sub>-adhA29C8 > P<sub>ADH1</sub>-ILV2**). The transcript levels
> of the **native ILV2, ILV5, ILV3, and ARO10** genes were also investigated, and as expected, the
> mRNA abundances of the native genes were **not statistically significantly altered** in any of
> the engineered strains compared to the WT strain."*

So: every cassette gene is expressed; **no native gene changes**. The authors could say that
because they mapped to a construct-augmented reference. Our index has native sequence only.

I pulled the deposited cassette (**MZ541859.1**), extracted the four codon-optimized CDS
(ILV2 4942–7005, ILV3 8273–10030, MLS-ARO10 13031–14998, ILV5 15944–17131) and compared each to
its native S288C transcript. The pipeline is `salmon 1.10.x`, decoy-aware gentrome, **`-k 31`**,
`--validateMappings`, default `minScoreFraction = 0.65`, and no read trimming
(`ops/userdata_mito.sh`). A read is assigned only if it contains an **exact 31-mer** shared with
an indexed transcript *and* clears the score threshold.

| cassette ORF | nt identity to native | **longest exact shared substring** | can seed at k=31? | modelled leak at 151 nt | observed gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ILV3** | 84.0 % | **44 nt** | **yes** | **12.00 %** | **+5,400 CPM** |
| ILV2 | 81.3 % | 32 nt | marginally | 0.00 % | 0 |
| **ILV5** | 86.4 % | **29 nt** | **no — misses by 2 nt** | **0.00 %** | **0** |
| **ARO10** | 77.6 % | **17 nt** | **no** | **0.00 %** | **0** |

The rank order of modelled leak is the rank order of observed signal, exactly. And the magnitude
closes: +5,415 CPM at a 12 % leak implies the synthetic pPGK1-ILV3 transcript sits near
45,000 CPM — entirely reasonable for the second-strongest promoter in the cassette
(native PGK1 is 33,294 CPM).

Note how fine the margin is. **ILV5 fails to seed by two nucleotides.** Had the codon optimizer
left a 31-nt stretch instead of 29, ILV5 would have behaved like ILV3 and the claim's premise
would have inverted. This is the whole of Claim 2's puzzle.

### 2.4 The alternatives the caller asked about, each disposed of

- **Salmon multimapping between construct and native locus** — rejected. If a cassette ORF were
  native-sequence, its reads would be *indistinguishable* from native and would be **added** to the
  native gene. Salmon cannot subtract. This mechanism can only ever inflate, never silence.
- **Transcript-level vs gene-level aggregation** — rejected. The FASTA has exactly one entry per
  gene of interest (one ARO10, one ILV2, one ILV5, one ILV3 among 6,471 transcripts) and the
  manifest records `unresolved_transcripts: 0` with 6,206 genes. Aggregation is 1:1.
- **The tTUB1 / tCYC1 / tTEF1 terminators and the promoters** — rejected. The index is
  CDS-only (`[partial=5',3']`), so no UTR sequence is indexed at all; a different terminator
  cannot change assignment of ORF-body reads.
- **The N-terminal truncation changing read assignment** — rejected, and it points the wrong way.
  ILV5Δ2-48 removes 144 nt of an 1,188 nt ORF: that is a ~12 % *deficit* in effective length,
  affecting TPM by ~12 % and counts essentially not at all. It cannot manufacture a 3-fold surplus.
- **The SRA genotype strings being wrong or swapped** — rejected for the *strains*. GEO's overall
  design and the paper independently confirm Y797 = mIBA<sup>ILV5</sup>, Y812 = cIBA<sup>ILV5</sup>,
  Y799 = cIBA<sup>IlvC6E6</sup>. The metadata error in this study is elsewhere: the **26 h
  sample-to-label assignment** (§1).
- **Unmapped fraction as evidence** — weakly supportive, not decisive. Producers run ~4 points
  below parent at 4 h (59.1 % vs 63.3 %) and 26 h, but not at 10 h. Too noisy to carry weight;
  the sequence comparison is what settles it.

### 2.5 One residual real finding

Native **ILV5 is genuinely elevated ~3-fold in both cytosolic strains** (Y799 +1.69, Y812 +1.55
log2 at 4 h; +1.83 / +2.47 at 10 h) and flat in Y797. Cross-mapping cannot produce it (0 % leak,
and Y799's KARI is *E. coli* with no yeast homology). It is **not** a general amino-acid-control
response: a 32-gene Gcn4/GAAC panel has median log2FC of −0.21 / −0.24 at 4 h in the very strains
where ILV5 is +1.6, and the control panel sits at −0.13 / −0.03. It is ILV5-specific and
genotype-linked.

This sits in tension with the paper's blanket statement that native genes are unaltered, and fits
the paper's own metabolomics (cytosolic strains accumulate 2-acetolactate 22–23× and DHIV
8.6–16×; native mitochondrial Ilv5p is the enzyme spanning that step). **It is worth flagging as a
discrepancy to check against their File S1, not as a settled contradiction** — their contrast
structure and normalization (edgeR/TMM) differ from ours.

Aside, also checked and worth recording: the Aft1/Aft2 **iron regulon is induced in the
*mitochondrial* strain Y797 at 10 h** (FIT1 +3.6, FIT3 +2.0, FIT2 +1.9, FET3 +1.5, panel median
+0.72) and **not** in the cytosolic strains (median −0.21 / +0.01). The paper's iron-limitation
conclusion rests on proteomics (Atm1p, Yap5p targets) and the *fra2Δ* rescue, not on this regulon,
so this is not a contradiction — but anyone reusing this study to support a transcriptional
iron-starvation signature in the cytosolic strains would be reading it backwards.

## 3. Verdicts

### CLAIM 1 — "ILV3 overexpression is visible and consistent" → **REFUTED**

Every number reproduces. The interpretation does not survive.

**Native ILV3 is not overexpressed.** The paper, mapping to a construct-augmented reference,
reports native ILV3 unchanged in all engineered strains. The signal at `YJR016C` in our matrix is
**~12 % spillover from the codon-optimized synthetic pPGK1-ILV3 transcript**, which retains a
44-nt exact match to the native ORF — long enough to seed salmon's k=31 index, where the
synthetic ILV5 (29 nt) and ARO10 (17 nt) are not.

Two of the cited figures are additionally contaminated: the 26 h "parent 199/235" and
"producers 2,173–3,160" come from the label-shifted block (§1).

**Correct statement:** *the `YJR016C` row separates cassette-bearing strains from the parent with a
clean >7-fold gap at every timepoint and a >25-fold gap at 10 h, and is therefore a reliable
**presence/absence marker for the isobutanol cassette**. Its magnitude is a cross-mapping artifact
and must not be reported as ILV3 expression, converted to a fold-change, or entered into any
differential-expression claim about native ILV3.*

### CLAIM 2 — "only ILV3 raises its transcript in Y797" → **REFUTED**

True as arithmetic about Y797; false as an inference, in two independent ways.

1. It is not Y797-specific. ILV2 and ARO10 are unelevated in **all three** producers.
2. The ILV5 contrast is confounded. **Y799 has no ScILV5 and shows the same elevation as Y812**,
   so truncation cannot be the cause.

**There is a mapping/quantification explanation, and it is complete and quantitative.** All
cassette ORFs are codon-optimized; our native-only index can only see them via spurious seeding.
The explanatory variable is the longest exact substring shared with the native ORF against
salmon's `k=31`: ILV3 44 nt → 12 % leak; ILV2 32 nt → 0 %; ILV5 29 nt → 0 %; ARO10 17 nt → 0 %,
despite pTDH3-ARO10 being the most abundant transcript in the cassette.

**Correct statement:** *this matrix cannot measure cassette expression in SRP342112 at all. The
four cassette ORFs with native homologues are codon-optimized and absent from the index; three
contribute exactly nothing to their native gene's counts and one (ILV3) contributes a ~12 %
spillover that masquerades as overexpression. Absence of signal at ILV2, ILV5 and ARO10 is
absence of a measurement, not absence of expression — the paper detects all five synthetic
transcripts and reports all four native genes unchanged. Any cassette-expression question about
this study requires requantification against a construct-augmented index built from
GenBank MZ541859.1 plus the ilvC6E6 and Δ-MLS variants.*

### CLAIM 3 — "SRR16481343 is an outlier or a mislabel" → **CONFIRMED**, and understated

It is not an outlier within its group; it is **not a 26 h parent sample at all**. Global ρ to its
declared cell is 0.752 against 0.949–0.987 for every other run; its best-matching cell is
**Y812 @ 10 h at ρ = 0.974**, replicate-grade. Growth-phase genes agree independently. Robust
z of its coherence margin is **−34.8**.

And the mislabel is not isolated: **the whole 26 h block is shifted by one position**, with
SRR16481346 (declared Y797) being a parent and SRR16481349 (declared Y799) being mitochondrial-class
by markers — corroborated by a drop in within-group dispersion for both groups (§1).

### CLAIM 3b — "this single run is why every 26 h contrast returned 0 genes" → **REFUTED**

Re-run with the repo's own method (median-of-ratios size factors, Welch t on
log2(normalized count + 1), BH across ~5,800 genes):

| 26 h Y795 vs … | declared | drop 343 | drop 343+346+349 | full marker relabel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Y797 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0** |
| Y799 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0** |
| Y812 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0** |

**Zero under every rule, including a perfect relabelling.** And the same test at other timepoints:

| | Y797 | Y799 | Y812 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 h | **0** | **0** | 356 |
| 10 h | **1** | 721 | 22 |

The 4 h Y795-vs-Y797 contrast returns 0 genes with three clean replicates per side and no
mislabelling anywhere near it. So the zero yield is a property of **the test**, not of
SRR16481343: a per-gene Welch t at n=2–3 with no dispersion shrinkage, BH-corrected across ~5,800
genes, has almost no power. The paper recovered hundreds of DE genes from the same data using
**edgeR** (negative-binomial GLM with shared-dispersion shrinkage) — that difference, not one bad
run, is what separates 0 from hundreds.

SRR16481343 *does* inflate variance badly (Y795 @ 26 h median within-group SD 0.188 → **0.638**,
3.4×). It should be removed. Just do not expect removing it to produce genes.

## 4. Recommended rule

A one-off exclusion of SRR16481343 would have caught one of the three mislabelled 26 h runs and
would still have left a parent sitting inside the Y797 group. Two complementary gates are needed,
both computable for any study with ≥2 declared replicates in ≥2 cells.

### Gate A — replicate coherence (study-generic, no biology needed)

For each sample *s* in a study, on log2 CPM over genes with CPM ≥ 1 in ≥ 10 samples, Spearman:

```
best_in (s)  = max  rho(s, t)  over t in the same declared (strain x condition_context) cell
best_out(s)  = max  rho(s, t)  over t outside it
margin  (s)  = best_in(s) - best_out(s)
robust_z(s)  = (margin(s) - median(margin)) / (1.4826 * MAD(margin))
```

Flag when `robust_z < -3`. **Use `max`, not `mean`.** With `mean`, a single bad member drags down
its innocent cell-mates: the mean form flagged SRR16481344 (z = −19.2) and SRR16481345 (z = −18.4)
purely because SRR16481343 was in their group. The `max` form is immune — it asks only "does this
sample have at least one credible partner under its own label".

Requires ≥2 samples in the cell; with n=1 the gate abstains rather than guesses.

On SRP342112 this flags exactly two runs and nothing else:

| run | declared | margin | robust z | best partner anywhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SRR16481343 | Y795 T14-1 | −0.2146 | **−34.8** | Y812 T6-2 (**different timepoint**) |
| SRR16481349 | Y799 T14-1 | −0.0244 | **−4.2** | Y797 T14-2 (**different strain, same timepoint**) |

The identity of `best_out`'s cell classifies the fault for free: a different **condition context**
→ timepoint/treatment mislabel; a different **strain, same context** → genotype mislabel.

### Gate B — declared-genotype marker consistency

Gate A is necessary but not sufficient: it misses SRR16481346, because at 26 h the strain effect
is small relative to the growth-phase effect, so a parent hides comfortably among producers.

For any study whose declared genotype names an over-expressed or deleted gene **that resolves to a
row in the matrix**, test that gene for a within-study bimodal split and require each sample to
fall on its declared side:

1. Within a condition context, take the declared-positive and declared-negative sets for the gene.
2. Require a clean gap — e.g. `min(positive) / max(negative) > 4` — otherwise the marker is
   uninformative and the gate abstains.
3. Flag any sample on the wrong side of the gap.

On SRP342112, ILV3 at 26 h is bimodal with a 7.6-fold gap (284 vs 2,173); SRR16481346 is declared
cassette-positive and sits at 284. **Flagged.**

This gate also has a built-in safety property demonstrated here: it must abstain when the marker
cannot be trusted. ILV2, ILV5 and ARO10 all fail step 2 in this study (no gap at all), which is
exactly right — they are unmeasurable (§2). A gate that had "used" them would have declared every
producer a parent.

### Disposition, and the write boundary

Both gates **quarantine, never delete**. A flagged sample gets a `context_conflict` annotation
recording the gate, the statistic, and the cell it actually resembles; contrast enumeration skips
quarantined samples and records that it did. Per `PLAN.md` L.5, the tool computes and proposes;
**promotion to the sample table stays a curator command.**

### What the rule does to the rest of SRP342112

- **Flags 3 of 33 runs, all inside the 26 h block** (343, 346, 349 — Gate A finds 343 and 349,
  Gate B finds 346). It flags nothing at 4 h or 10 h, where the declared labels are internally
  consistent. No false positives.
- **26 h contrasts:** Y795 drops to n=2, Y797 to n=2, Y799 to n=2, Y812 stays n=2. All three
  Y795-vs-producer contrasts remain expressible at `MIN_UNITS = 2` — and all three still return
  **0 genes at FDR 5 %**, exactly as before (§3b). The rule buys correctness, not yield.
- **4 h and 10 h contrasts are untouched** — 356 / 721 / 22 genes as before.

### The separate, larger problem this exposes

The zero yield at 26 h and at 4 h Y797 is a **power** problem, not a metadata problem. If contrasts
at n=2–3 are meant to be informative, the Welch-t-per-gene design in
`src/fermdb/omics/contrasts.py` needs dispersion sharing across genes (the edgeR/DESeq2
empirical-Bayes step) before any n=2 or n=3 result should be read as "no difference". Until then,
a 0-gene result at n≤3 should be recorded as **uninformative**, not as a negative finding.

And for this study specifically: **`s288c.counts.tsv.gz` cannot answer any question about the
isobutanol cassette.** The runs should carry a flag saying so, pointing at MZ541859.1.
